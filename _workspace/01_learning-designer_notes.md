# Stage 1 - Learning designer notes

Assumptions: 7 cards, above-ground residential yard only, US English (Fahrenheit avoided, no numbers a child can't picture). Each card is placed at a different spot so hotspots spread across the yard.

## Real ant fact behind each card
- **heavy-cracker** (sidewalk): worker ants can carry roughly 10x their body weight (some species far more); food too big for one ant is dragged by groups (cooperative transport), which is slow and hard to steer.
- **nest-guard** (nest entrance, lawn edge): many yard ants (e.g. pavement ants) fight raids from neighboring colonies; guard ants at the entrance check nestmates by smell (colony odor on the body), and some beetles do sneak into ant nests.
- **rain-flood** (garden bed puddle by the hose): a large raindrop can weigh more than a small worker ant; heavy rain floods shallow tunnels; ants can survive underwater for a while by closing their breathing holes, but not indefinitely, and eggs/larvae are at risk.
- **hot-driveway** (driveway): dark asphalt in summer sun often reaches 140 F or more, too hot for bare feet; many common yard ants die at body temperatures around 113-122 F, which is why they forage in cooler hours.
- **lost-trail** (lawn between nest and trash bin): foragers lay pheromone trails to food that nestmates follow; water (rain, sprinklers) washes the trail away and ants fall back on slow searching. Worker ants are all female, so "sisters" is accurate.
- **giant-feet** (doormat at porch steps): ants sense ground vibration through organs in their legs, but cannot avoid fast footsteps; foot traffic is a real cause of ant deaths on paths.
- **sidewalk-gap** (sidewalk crack by the fence): worker ants are wingless and most species cannot jump; a gap wider than their reach forces a long climb down and up (some species even build living bridges with their bodies - not mentioned on the card so the answer is not given).

## Open questions for the teacher
1. "Your feet burn" (hot-driveway) and "the eggs and baby ants are in danger" (rain-flood) are the most dramatic lines. Are they OK for your class, or should they be softer?
2. The nest-guard card mentions enemy ants and a beetle, but no birds or animals, to keep the problem ant-sized. Do you also want a bigger animal (for example a curious bird) at the door, as in your maze-door example?
3. Should students see only these 7 cards, or do you want a few spare cards (for example a spider web on the fence) so more students can pick a unique problem?
4. The garden hose and the trash bin are named in two cards (rain-flood, lost-trail). The world-builder will need both objects in the scene.

## Stage 1 revision - teacher feedback: "add only the fence spider web"
Assumption: one new card (8th, at the end); the 7 existing cards are unchanged (script confirmed their counts match the table above).
- **fence-web** (low web where a fence post meets the bottom rail, garden bed end of the fence, away from the sidewalk crack): spiders are real ant predators; some yard spiders (e.g. cobweb spiders) build webs low on fences and walls with sticky threads that catch walking ants; spider silk is very strong for its thinness, and web spiders find prey by feeling the web vibrate. The card shows the ant stuck and the spider running toward it; nothing is eaten. The question asks for a way past the web, without hinting at an answer.
- Counts: problem 38 + whyHard 27 = 65 words (limit 70); longest sentence 14 words (limit 18); question 14 words, starts with "How could you".
- Open question: "Now the spider is running toward you!" is the most dramatic line. OK for your class, or should it be softer (for example "The spider feels the shaking")?

## Stage 1 revision 2 - teacher feedback: "students may think 'just go around the web'; give a reason the ant can't avoid it"
Changed only **fence-web** (id unchanged): title "Caught in a Web" -> "A Web in the Way"; new placeHint, problem, whyHard and question. Other 7 cards untouched (script counts identical).
- Fact check: ants are drawn to sweet, ripe fruit (fallen strawberries are a common ant food; sugar is a main energy source for workers). Spider silk is sticky and strong; web spiders sense prey by web vibration.
- Tone softened: the ant is no longer stuck and the spider no longer runs at it; the card only says the ant "could get stuck" and the spider "would feel the web shake". Matches the scene (friendly-looking spider, empty web).
